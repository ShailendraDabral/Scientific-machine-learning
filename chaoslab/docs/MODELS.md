# ChaosLab numerical core: models and methods

Generated by `npm run docs` from the metadata in `src/core`. Do not edit by hand.

## Integrators

| Method | id | Order | Symplectic | Evaluations per step | Stability limit on dt x rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forward Euler | `euler` | 1 | no | 1 | 2 | Explicit, first order. Gains energy on oscillatory problems at any step size; provided as a baseline. |
| Explicit midpoint (RK2) | `midpoint` | 2 | no | 2 | 2 | Explicit, second order. Weakly unstable for purely oscillatory problems (amplitude grows as h^4 per step). |
| Runge-Kutta 4 | `rk4` | 4 | no | 4 | 2.785 | Explicit, fourth order. Default for dissipative systems. Energy drifts slowly and steadily in conservative systems. |
| Symplectic Euler | `symplecticEuler` | 1 | yes | 1 | 2 | First order, symplectic. Requires acceleration that depends on position and time only. Bounded O(h) energy error. |
| Stormer-Verlet | `verlet` | 2 | yes | 2 | 2 | Second order, symplectic, time-reversible. Requires acceleration that depends on position and time only. Bounded O(h^2) energy error. |

The stability limit is the linear one (test equation y' = lambda y). "rate" is a per-system upper bound on the fastest local time scale (`rateScale`). It is a necessary condition, not a guarantee: nonlinear systems can still diverge below it, which the solver catches by checking every step for non-finite or runaway values.

Symplectic Euler and Stormer-Verlet are offered only where H = T(p) + V(q): the three-body problem always, the Duffing oscillator when delta = 0. They are refused for the double pendulum, whose kinetic energy depends on the angles.

## Models

### Lorenz system

```text
dx/dt = sigma (y - x)
dy/dt = x (rho - z) - y
dz/dt = x y - beta z
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sigma | 10 | > 0 | Prandtl number |
| rho | 28 | >= 0 | reduced Rayleigh number |
| beta | 2.66667 | > 0 | geometric factor |

**Assumptions**

- A three-mode Galerkin truncation of Rayleigh-Benard convection (Boussinesq fluid between two plates); x is convective intensity, y and z are temperature-field modes.
- Variables and time are non-dimensional. sigma > 0, beta > 0 and rho >= 0 are required because they are a Prandtl number, a geometric factor and a Rayleigh ratio.
- Deterministic and autonomous: no noise and no explicit time dependence.

**Limitations**

- It is not a quantitative model of real convection: the truncation is only justified near onset (rho slightly above 1). Chaos at rho = 28 is a property of the ODE, not of the fluid.
- In the chaotic regime (largest Lyapunov exponent about 0.906 at the defaults) round-off grows by e every 1.1 time units. In double precision an individual trajectory is not pointwise meaningful beyond roughly 40 time units; attractor geometry and statistics remain valid.
- Explicit methods need dt * (local rate) below their stability limit; Forward Euler diverges for dt around 0.03 and above at the default parameters.

### Rossler system

```text
dx/dt = -y - z
dy/dt = x + a y
dz/dt = b + z (x - c)
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | 0.2 | finite | linear growth rate in the x-y plane |
| b | 0.2 | finite | constant injection into z |
| c | 5.7 | finite | threshold of the z nonlinearity |

**Assumptions**

- A minimal abstract model of chaos with a single quadratic nonlinearity (z*x); it was designed, not derived from a physical system.
- Variables and time are non-dimensional; the system is deterministic and autonomous.
- The well-studied regime is a, b, c > 0; the defaults a = b = 0.2, c = 5.7 give the classic funnel-free spiral attractor.

**Limitations**

- The attractor has a bounded basin. Initial conditions outside it, and many parameter choices, give trajectories that escape to infinity; the solver stops with a NumericalInstabilityError when that happens.
- Phase-volume contraction (a + x - c) depends on position, and the z spikes are fast: the local rate reaches |x - c| + |z|, which sets the step-size limit.
- Chaos is weak (largest Lyapunov exponent about 0.071 at the defaults), so long runs are needed before chaotic statistics converge.

### Logistic map

```text
x[n+1] = r x[n] (1 - x[n])
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r | 3.9 | 0 to 4 | growth rate |

**Assumptions**

- Discrete, non-overlapping generations; x is population divided by carrying capacity, so x lies in [0, 1].
- r in [0, 4] is required: only then does the map send [0, 1] into itself. Outside this range orbits leave the interval and diverge to minus infinity.
- Deterministic, one-dimensional, no age structure, no noise.

**Limitations**

- A conceptual model of period doubling and chaos, not a quantitative population model.
- In floating point every orbit is eventually periodic, because only finitely many doubles exist in [0, 1]. Such cycles are typically many millions of iterates long, far beyond the runs used here, but statements about the infinite-time limit are approximations.
- In chaotic regimes the error doubles roughly every iterate at r = 4 (lambda = ln 2), so after about 50 iterates the computed x_n is unrelated to the exact orbit of x0; invariant density and Lyapunov exponent remain reliable.
- Initial values 0, 1 and exact preimages of fixed points give degenerate orbits that are not typical.

### Duffing oscillator

```text
x'' + delta x' + alpha x + beta x^3 = gamma cos(omega t)

first-order form, state [x, v]:
x' = v
v' = -delta v - alpha x - beta x^3 + gamma cos(omega t)
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| alpha | -1 | finite | linear stiffness |
| beta | 1 | finite | cubic stiffness |
| delta | 0.3 | >= 0 | linear damping |
| gamma | 0.5 | finite | drive amplitude |
| omega | 1.2 | >= 0 | drive angular frequency |

**Assumptions**

- A single degree of freedom with unit mass, linear viscous damping (delta >= 0), a stiffness with one cubic correction, and a harmonic drive of fixed amplitude and frequency.
- alpha < 0 with beta > 0 is the double-well (buckled beam) case; alpha > 0 with beta > 0 is a hardening spring.
- Time is explicit in the drive, so the phase space is (x, v, phase); Poincare sections are taken stroboscopically once per drive period.

**Limitations**

- The cubic term is the first nonlinear term of a symmetric restoring force and is only accurate for moderate amplitudes of the physical system it stands for.
- With beta < 0 the potential is unbounded below and trajectories can escape; the solver reports this as a NumericalInstabilityError.
- Symplectic integrators are valid only for delta = 0 and are refused otherwise. Energy is a conserved quantity only for delta = 0 and gamma = 0.
- For exact stroboscopic sampling choose dt = T / N with integer N; otherwise the section needs interpolation.

### Double pendulum

```text
state [theta1, theta2, omega1, omega2], D = theta1 - theta2

omega1' = [ -g (2 m1 + m2) sin(theta1) - m2 g sin(theta1 - 2 theta2)
            - 2 m2 sin(D) (omega2^2 l2 + omega1^2 l1 cos(D)) ] / [ l1 (2 m1 + m2 - m2 cos(2D)) ]

omega2' = [ 2 sin(D) ( omega1^2 l1 (m1 + m2) + g (m1 + m2) cos(theta1)
            + omega2^2 l2 m2 cos(D) ) ] / [ l2 (2 m1 + m2 - m2 cos(2D)) ]

E = (m1+m2) l1^2 omega1^2 / 2 + m2 l2^2 omega2^2 / 2 + m2 l1 l2 omega1 omega2 cos(D)
    - (m1+m2) g l1 cos(theta1) - m2 g l2 cos(theta2)
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| m1 | 1 | > 0 | upper bob mass (kg) |
| m2 | 1 | > 0 | lower bob mass (kg) |
| l1 | 1 | > 0 | upper rod length (m) |
| l2 | 1 | > 0 | lower rod length (m) |
| g | 9.81 | >= 0 | gravitational acceleration (m/s^2) |

**Assumptions**

- Two point masses on rigid, massless rods, moving in a vertical plane under uniform gravity.
- Frictionless pivots and no air drag: the system is conservative and total energy is constant.
- Angles are measured from the downward vertical and are kept unwrapped, so full rotations accumulate multiples of 2 pi.
- SI units by default (kg, m, s); masses and lengths must be > 0, g >= 0.

**Limitations**

- Real pendulums have distributed mass, bearing friction and drag; this model overstates how long motion persists and does not include rod inertia.
- The Hamiltonian is not separable, so Symplectic Euler and Stormer-Verlet are refused. Forward Euler, midpoint and RK4 do not conserve energy; the relative energy drift reported by the solver is the accuracy measure.
- Motion is regular at low energy and chaotic at intermediate and high energy; in chaotic regimes nearby trajectories separate exponentially and long individual trajectories are not reproducible across step sizes or methods.
- Fast rotations raise the local rate (|omega|), which tightens the step-size limit.

### Three-body problem

```text
r_i'' = G sum_{j != i} m_j (r_j - r_i) / (|r_j - r_i|^2 + eps^2)^(3/2)

E = sum m_i |v_i|^2 / 2 - G sum_{i<j} m_i m_j / r_ij
P = sum m_i v_i
L = sum m_i r_i x v_i
```

**Parameters**

| Name | Default | Valid range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | 1 | > 0 | gravitational constant |
| softening | 0 | >= 0 | Plummer softening length eps |
| dim | 2 | 2 or 3 | spatial dimension, 2 or 3 |
| masses | [1, 1, 1] | each > 0, at least two | body masses, each > 0 |

**Assumptions**

- Point masses interacting only through Newtonian inverse-square gravity; no relativity, tides, radiation, collisions or mergers.
- An isolated system: total energy, linear momentum and angular momentum are conserved, and the centre of mass moves uniformly.
- Units are non-dimensional with G = 1 by default; masses must be > 0. The code accepts any N >= 2 bodies in 2 or 3 dimensions.
- Softening eps = 0 by default (exact Newtonian force). A non-zero eps replaces r^2 by r^2 + eps^2 and is reported as a warning because it changes the physics.

**Limitations**

- The force is singular at zero separation. Close encounters need steps far smaller than the rest of the orbit; with a fixed step the solver warns when dt * sqrt(G (m_i + m_j) / r^3) exceeds the stability limit, and an exact collision raises a NumericalInstabilityError. No regularisation is implemented.
- Generic three-body motion is chaotic and often ends with one body escaping; long individual trajectories are not reproducible across step sizes or methods.
- Stormer-Verlet and Symplectic Euler keep the energy error bounded and conserve momentum and angular momentum to round-off, but only at fixed step. Euler, midpoint and RK4 show secular energy drift.
- Positions grow without bound after an escape, which reduces floating-point resolution of the remaining pair.
